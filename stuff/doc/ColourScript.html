Colour Script

Shift the colours of a whole scene for the colour script of a film: dusk, night, a flashback, a mood that changes across the cuts. Add Colour Script from the Image Adjust category and place it between the Xsheet node and the Output, so that every column goes through it, or after only some columns.

Shift

Hue turns the hues by an angle, from -180 to 180 degrees. Saturation goes from -100 (grey) through 0 (unchanged) to 100 (fully saturated): in between, each colour's saturation moves that share of the way. Lightness works the same way from black to white. Every value can be animated: keys at the first frame of each cut give each cut its own look, and keys further apart blend from one look into another.

How the colours change

For Toonz Vector and Toonz Raster levels the shift is made on a copy of the palette when the column is rendered: every colour of every style changes, the level's own palette is never modified, and lines and fills stay as clean as they are. Full-colour levels (images, raster drawings) have no palette, so their pixels are shifted instead. The viewer shows the shift of a Colour Script after the Xsheet node on the palette levels when the preference that shows the Colour Override in the viewer is on.

Keep

Keep Styles is a prefix: the styles whose name starts with it keep their colours, so a character can stay on model while the set around it changes (name its styles "KEEP Skin", "KEEP Hair", and type KEEP). To keep a whole column, right-click its header and choose Toggle Keep Colours.

Notes

A Colour Script after another one shifts the colours again. Presets of the Fx (right-click the node, Save As Preset) keep a look to reuse in other scenes. The math is the same for 8-bit, 16-bit and floating-point renders.
